

With a need to find new technologies to be used in modern systems, it's important to know where this is already being research and implemented. One such development is with the integration of AI in software and systems. The integration of AI into engineering solutions in embedded systems has been done across various domains, including healthcare (Aziz _et al._, 2026), industrial IoT (Dini _et al._, 2024) and in electric and hybrid vehicles (Waheed and Abro, 2026). The domain that will be the focus of this thesis is in military systems with the use case of Suppression of Enemy Air Defense (SEAD).
SEAD is a mission type that aims to enable friendly air superiority and freedom of movement by disruption or destruction of enemy air defense. Enemy threats improve in their technical complexity and require an answer through technological developments on friendly side as well. One form of a response is in the use of unmanned platforms, which in combination with other technologies turn the battlefield into a complex, asymmetric environment. (Speed & Stathopoulos, 2018)
In such an environment, unmanned platforms must be able to react to changing conditions without requiring manual intervention by human control. For this, decision-making capabilities must be on board the platform itself, which is where automated planning enters the picture. In this work, it will be analyzed for the domain of avionic systems, which is a subdomain of embedded systems, how AI planning can be used in the context of SEAD with a hierarchical task network (HTN) in a safety-critical system. HTNs are well suited in domains where a hierarchical representation is desirable (Georgievski and Aiello, 2015). The hierarchical model can also be seen in military structures and organization across strategy, operations and tactics (Nagele, 2023) which is a good match with HTN.
A common challenge in safety-critical systems is component or system failure (Maurya and Kumar, 2020), which requires foresight in the design phase during development and an approach must be defined to mitigate its effects with recovery and fallback mechanisms. Software and hardware systems in avionics commonly use triple-modular redundancy (TMR) already (Yeh, 1996; Ahlstrom et al., 2002; Martin et al., 2015) which creates the possibility to use AI planning with an established redundancy solution.

Firstly, the definition of what an HTN is will be introduced and then applied to an example that demonstrates a scenario applicable to SEAD. After this, the topic of redundancy engineering and fault-tolerance will be introduced and why it is required. Then connecting both topics, a concept will be developed that uses redundantly designed HTN planning.
